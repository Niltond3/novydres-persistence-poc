# J1 — Coterie Canonical Baseline Research

## A. Scope and authority

This artifact is a **research baseline**, not a Novydres coterie-design contract.

J1 recovers and normalizes canonical V5 evidence concerning coteries, coterie creation, coterie types, shared traits, Domain, Advantages/Flaws and related group structures. It does **not** resolve `CHAR-CREATE-Q-117–125`, create `CHAR-J-*` decisions, select a Novydres workflow, design shared history, adapt Domain, define a Relationship Map procedure, or define a final coterie output schema.

The normalization posture is inherited from Specialist A. Specialist A explicitly requires J1 to consume hybrid admission granularity, stable option identity and semantic supersession. fileciteturn0file1L113-L125 The relevant source states are `ADMITTED / SUPERSEDED / PARTIAL / EXCLUDED`, while unresolved transcription is tracked separately as `SOURCE POINTER DEBT`. fileciteturn1file7L443-L466

C already distinguishes current placement from feeding/domain rights and keeps social relationships from automatically granting mechanical dots. fileciteturn0file2L142-L157

Power Scale constructs such as Collective Power Profile, Complementarity, Coordination, Shared Resources, Collective Reach, Critical Dependencies and Collective Threat Vector are **Novydres-local audit structures**, not canonical coterie mechanics unless an independent V5 source establishes an analogue. ## C. Canonical coterie baseline

### C.1 What a coterie is

Canonical V5 treats the coterie as the **group of vampires who function together in the chronicle**, with mechanically representable shared traits and, where applicable, shared Domain. The current Core product description explicitly places the coterie and its jointly controlled domain inside character creation and notes that coterie types differ in advantages, disadvantages and tasks. citeturn489299search14

This establishes three baseline facts for J1:

1. coterie is not merely a narrative label;
2. it can own or express group-level mechanical state;
3. coterie construction is canonically adjacent to character creation rather than an unrelated later subsystem.

The canonical corpus also treats coteries as purpose-bearing formations. *Cults of the Blood Gods* describes coteries forming for self-interest, shared beliefs and mutual survival, then supplies specialized examples. fileciteturn5file3L694-L726

## D. Canonical creation procedure

### D.1 Stable structure

The canonical Core baseline makes coterie creation a **collaborative group-building step associated with character creation**. The coterie receives a group resource/dot economy, and player characters can contribute from individual Advantage allocation into shared coterie traits. Coterie Flaws can also participate in that group economy. The precise current Core formula and small-group exception require page verification before publication-quality transcription; J1 therefore records the structure as canonical and the numeric transcription as `SOURCE POINTER DEBT`.

The Player's Guide does not merely add isolated options: its official description says it **revisits character and coterie creation** and supplies new systems and coterie advantages. citeturn489299search4 This is sufficient to treat the PG as a required normalization source, but not sufficient to infer that every Core coterie rule was replaced.

### D.2 What creation can produce canonically

A coterie can canonically emerge with some combination of:

- a type/archetypal purpose;
- shared Domain, if the coterie has one;
- Domain dimensions such as Chasse, Lien and Portillon;
- shared Backgrounds/Advantages and associated assets;
- group-level Flaws/liabilities;
- optional additions suggested by its type;
- later Player's Guide coterie Advantages, including clan coterie merits where eligible.

Expansion examples demonstrate that this is a **profile**, not a rule that every coterie must possess every category. Saboteurs, for example, are presented without a default Domain and list Domain only as a possible extra if the group becomes embedded in a locale. fileciteturn5file1L280-L313 Therefore “coterie” and “shared Domain” are canonically related but not identical concepts.

### D.3 PCs versus broader applicability

The **baseline creation economy is player-character-facing**. J1 does not generalize its character-contribution formula to NPC construction without an explicit source rule.

At the same time, the broader V5 corpus clearly uses coterie types and group mechanical profiles to describe Kindred groups as setting entities, including sourcebook coteries whose purpose and resources are not limited to a player-only ontology. Thus:

- `CANONICAL FINDING`: coterie as an in-world group structure has broader setting applicability;
- `SOURCE POINTER DEBT / PROCEDURE BOUNDARY`: the exact PC creation allocation procedure must not be assumed to be the canonical NPC-building procedure.

This distinction is directly relevant to Novydres because Quick/Guided may create PCs or NPCs, but J1 makes no local procedural decision from it.

### D.4 Session-zero / chronicle setup positioning

Canonical evidence places coterie creation near character and chronicle setup and treats it as collaborative. The Player's Guide explicitly revisits both character and coterie creation. citeturn489299search21 J1 did **not** recover primary text establishing one mandatory universal session-zero order that must be copied into Novydres. Exact sequence is therefore a J2 decision informed by canon, not a J1 conclusion.

## G. Domain-related mechanics

### G.1 Domain structure

Canonical V5 Domain is a coterie-scale territorial mechanic described through the dimensions **Chasse, Lien and Portillon**. Later official coterie types repeatedly use these dimensions in their mechanical profiles, confirming that they remain active V5 vocabulary rather than abandoned early-edition terminology. Family and Gatekeepers both receive all three dimensions in *Cults*. fileciteturn5file2L478-L528 Envoys and Think Tanks use Domain profiles with Chasse/Lien. fileciteturn5file3L711-L758

At a high level:

- **Chasse** represents the Domain's value/quality for hunting and feeding;
- **Lien** represents the coterie's degree of connection, integration or control within the Domain;
- **Portillon** represents security against intrusion and the strength of the Domain's boundaries/defenses.

Exact dice modifiers, feeding calculations, deterioration/maintenance procedures and current Player's Guide refinements are `SOURCE POINTER DEBT` and must be page-verified before standalone publication text is written.

### G.2 Domain is not mandatory for every coterie

Canonical expansion evidence is explicit that some coterie profiles may lack a default Domain. Saboteurs list Domain as an optional extra only if the group is embedded in its current locale. fileciteturn5file1L290-L313 Therefore:

`CANONICAL FINDING` — **coterie existence does not require that every coterie begin with a mechanically rated shared Domain.**

This is evidence for J2, not a Novydres implementation decision.

### G.3 Domain versus member ownership

A shared coterie Domain is a group-level trait. J1 finds no basis to infer that its dots automatically create separate individual legal title, residence, refuge ownership, feeding permission, office, Status, Bairrismo or Marco Territorial for every member. Those are distinct local Novydres surfaces under Specialist C. C explicitly separates current placement from residence/refuge/feeding/domain/ownership/Marco and states that narrative relations do not automatically create mechanical traits. fileciteturn0file8L672-L683

## H. Advantages / Flaws / shared assets

### H.1 Coterie Advantages and shared Backgrounds

Canonical coterie construction can hold shared Backgrounds/Advantages and can receive them through coterie-type profiles. Official expansion examples include social connections, wealth, bases, patrons, influence, servants and identity infrastructure. This proves a broad **shared-resource architecture**, but it does not mean every individually legal Background is automatically legal as a shared coterie trait in every context.

Player's Guide materially expands this layer with **coterie advantages and clan coterie merits**. The official publisher description confirms that this family covers all thirteen main clans plus Caitiff, Duskborn and Salubri. citeturn489299search4 J1 records the family as canonical evidence; exact merit identities, prerequisites, stacking/application and member-benefit rules require PG page verification.

### H.2 Coterie Flaws

Canonical type profiles can carry Flaws/liabilities at group level. *Cults* examples attach enemies/adversaries, Status liabilities, No Haven or Dark Secret-style complications to coteries. fileciteturn5file0L90-L99 fileciteturn5file1L299-L313

`SOURCE POINTER DEBT` — J1 has not transcribed the current Core's exact universal coterie-Flaw dot-economy wording. The existence of group-level liabilities and their role in the coterie creation economy is established; exact numeric formulas must be verified against the current Core/PG.

### H.3 Shared patron/enemy/obligation structures

Canon supports shared patron/enemy-like mechanics through ordinary Background/Flaw families used at coterie level. For example, Saboteurs can have a Mawla linked to their mission and an Adversary; Nemeses carry an Enemy; Family coteries carry Enemies. fileciteturn5file1L290-L313 fileciteturn5file2L478-L504

However, J1 recovered **no universal canonical generator** that requires every coterie to create a patron, enemy and obligation trio, nor a mandatory shared-history procedure that generates them. That remains J2 design space under Q-122.
